## THE DECISIVE INTFER-SIDE FACT: there is no device softmax kernel to fuse into

This is what rejects candidate 1 on its own, before the backend wall is even
consulted, so it is recorded first:

- include/intfer/attention/flash_attention.hpp:9-25 says verbatim that the two
  FlashAttention SYCL kernels are "DEMO/REFERENCE-ONLY ... NOT reachable from the
  inference path", their only call sites are examples/flash_attention_demo/ and
  examples/qwen_forward_gpu/, and src/engine/engine.cpp:33 is "a bare #include
  with ZERO calls to either symbol".
- intfer's decode softmax is a HOST scalar loop: examples/intfer_cli/main.cpp:5580-5622
  (score pass + expf softmax + weighted-sum, reading K/V through `kv_row_ptr`).
- its prefill softmax is the same host loop: main.cpp:6291-6321.
- the KV it reads is host FP32: `std::vector<float> kcache, vcache` at
  main.cpp:4290 (struct Layer, "Per-layer state (host-resident)", main.cpp:4284).
- the 27B is 16 full-attn + 48 GDN/SSM layers and the decode hot path is the
  GatedDeltaNet SSM recurrence, not softmax attention.

So a CUDA paged-attention kernel has no seam here even if the backend wall did
not exist. The GQA-packing note pulsarlm carries (paged_attention.cu:21-23, "the
`group` query heads sharing g occupy MMA rows 0..group-1") is already closed in
this repo's own record: flash_attention.hpp:24-25 says a device paged-KV
softmax-decode kernel "does not exist in this tree", and HISTORY.md records
"GQA-packing DECLINED with evidence".

## REJECTED CANDIDATES (each with its reason)

### 1. Fused per-page attention mass
- What it does: `accumulate_group_mass` (paged_attention.cu:288-312) adds each
  attended key's normalised weight exp(score - lse[m]) times the sequence's mass
  gain into the key's mass row; pass 1 stashes the scaled scores
  (paged_attention.cu:270-272, `seq_scores[...] = ssc`) so the mass pass never
  re-reads K, and prefill does an explicit second QKᵀ pass to build the same
  per-key mass (paged_prefill.cuh:322-360, "Pass 2: recompute QKᵀ and accumulate
  per-key mass").
- intfer seam: NONE — there is no device attention kernel to fuse the accumulator
  into (see the block above); intfer's softmax is a host scalar loop.
- The mass exists to drive page EVICTION, and intfer has no eviction consumer:
  `kv_require_cap` is a hard abort, not a policy (main.cpp:4496-4502,
  "[intfer] FATAL: pos %d >= KV capacity %d", `std::exit(1)` at :4500).
- Round trips/launches removed: 0 — arithmetic-only, so it fails the standing
  fusion test on both counts.
- Weight-format wall / no-P2P: CUDA-only backend; no P2P analogue in intfer.
  Blocked by no-P2P: N/A (intfer has no P2P either, but the rejection does not
  rest on that).
- Effort: L. Already shipped: NO (rejected).

### 2. Bounded-context eviction policy — REJECTED as a port, and the ONE genuine capability gap
- What it does: `pages_to_evict` (eviction.cpp:48-92) keeps the first n_sink and
  last n_working pages, corrects each page's EMA density by the age factor
  `1 - (1 - attention_mass_decay)^(forwarded_count - first_query)` (:71-73), bars
  anything below `evict_bar_from_tau` (:65, the tau/(1-tau) bar, eviction.hpp:27-29),
  then drops the lowest-density pages until at most max_pages remain (:81-89).
  Policy struct at eviction.hpp:11-25 (n_sink=64, n_working=512, evict_tau=0.0 by
  default — "The defaults evict nothing", eviction.hpp:12-15).
- Why it is a genuine gap: intfer has NO forgetting design at all. It has the
  opposite — a hard abort once positions reach the KV capacity (main.cpp:4497-4500).
- Why still rejected as a port: intfer's PrefixCache already solves the
  bounded-context problem it was written for, by snapshot/resume rather than by
  dropping pages (main.cpp:9254-9479, `INTFER_PCACHE_N` default 4 clamped 1..16 at
  :9282-9291, kill switch `INTFER_NO_PREFIX_CACHE` at :9292-9295).
- Round trips/launches removed: 0 — host-side bookkeeping only.
- Weight-format wall / no-P2P: CUDA-only backend. Effort: S.

### 3. Pinned-host-memory LRU state pool
- What it does: `host_page_pool` (host_page_pool.hpp:15-19) is pinned host memory
  managed as a buddy allocator; `pinned_int64` (kv_cache.cpp:140-147) allocates
  with `at::TensorOptions().dtype(at::kLong).pinned_memory(true)`; the async
  device<->host movement is `cudaMemcpyAsync` (kv_cache.cpp:392-398 H2D,
  :419-426 D2H) plus the vectorised `copy_segments_cuda` kernel
  (page_copy.cu:14-40, one CTA per segment, a uint4 path at :27-33).
- Why rejected: it accelerates DMA between device KV and PAGEABLE host. intfer's
  KV is ALREADY host-resident and its cross-device data movement is shared-USM
  host reads/writes and host memcpy, not DMA off pageable memory:
  engine.cpp:1649-1650 states it verbatim -- "(one wide memcpy, not scalar
  half-stores into malloc_shared)" -- and the optional activation-dedup path
  INTFER_FUSE_ACT (default OFF, engine.cpp:1587-1600) removes a second UPLOAD of
  identical bits, which is the opposite direction from what pinning would buy.
  There is nothing for pinning to accelerate.
- Measured, not assumed: `malloc_host` / `pinned_memory` appears NOWHERE in
  intfer's src/ or include/ — the allocator in use everywhere is
  `sycl::malloc_shared`.
- Round trips/launches removed: 0. Weight-format wall / no-P2P: CUDA-only backend.
  Effort: M. Already shipped: NO (rejected).

### 4. Snapshot/resume of context to an external store
- What it does: state is identified by a page-hash chain — `state_key`
  (pulsarlm/src/pulsarlm/store.py:18-24) takes "the last page hash and the tail",
  where "the newest page is never evicted, and its hash chains every page before
  it"; the store keeps stored pages/states in an OrderedDict LRU with
  fetch/lease/write (store.py:31-44, 108-229) and can spill to a file
  (store_file.py, store.py:87-105 `_load`).
- Why rejected as ALREADY-SHIPPED-EQUIVALENT: intfer's PrefixCache
  (main.cpp:9254-9479) is a bounded multi-entry LRU holding per-layer KV plus the
  INT8/Q8 mirrors and the SSM/conv recurrent grid snapshots
  (`Entry::LayerSnap` at main.cpp:9259-9273, `std::map<int, std::vector<PcSsm>> grid`
  at :9274), with `INTFER_PCACHE_N` and `INTFER_NO_PREFIX_CACHE`. Nothing to port.
  (The first-pass block above recorded this as "no speculative decoding machinery"
  in intfer, which is wrong: speculative decode exists — see the survey below —
  and either way the snapshot/resume half maps onto PrefixCache, not onto the
  draft model.)
- Round trips/launches removed: 0. Effort: N/A.

### 5. Flash-decode split+combine, and GQA packing into MMA rows 0..group-1
- What it does: the split path spreads one (s, g)'s view over nsplits CTAs
  (paged_attention.cu:377-388 `paged_decode_split_kernel`) and combines the
  per-split (o, m, l) partials; the GQA shape packs the `group` query heads
  sharing kv head g into MMA rows 0..group-1 with rows group..15 zeroed and
  discarded (paged_attention.cu:21-23).
- Why rejected: no device attention seam in intfer (see the decisive fact above),
  and the GQA-packing shape was already DECLINED with evidence in this repo's own
  survey (vllm-xpu `decode_policy_qpacked_head` / `qgroup`): the heading is
  HISTORY.md:1236-1237 ("GQA-packing DECLINED with evidence (path (b), not
  shipped)") and the evidence trail is HISTORY.md:1308-1313 ("DECLINED WITH
  EVIDENCE, not shipped" -- `INTFER_DECODE1_GQAPACK` "does not exist in the tree
  and will not be added"), corroborated by the note at flash_attention.hpp:24-25.
- Round trips/launches removed: 0. Effort: L.

## THE DECISIVE GENERAL FACTS (all three apply)

- The DashQ INT2 weight-format wall applies: intfer's weights are packed INT2 codes
  + per-(row, K-group) FP16 scales + a host global divisor; any port of a foreign
  weight-layout kernel is a re-quantisation of the whole 27B, not a
  micro-optimisation.
- No-P2P applies: intfer has no P2P/IPC (only comments mention it), so no
  cross-device collective has a consumer.
- This engine's KV design is the OPPOSITE of what intfer is optimising for. PulsarLM's
  whole transfer-reduction apparatus (pinned staging pool, async page DMA, page
  reuse) exists because its KV lives on the DEVICE and pages must move. intfer's
  objective is the reverse — get the intermediate OFF the host and keep work
  device-resident — so those ideas point the wrong way for this tree.

## CONCLUSION (second survey)

0 candidates, nothing transfers. The first-pass per-candidate line ranges above
are superseded by this block where they disagree; its one factual error (the
"no speculative decoding machinery" line) is annotated in place rather than
rewritten. Closing reason, in one sentence: a CUDA-only single-GPU device-resident-paged-KV
engine has neither the backend nor the device-attention seam nor the memory
objective this tree is optimising for, and its one genuine capability gap
(eviction policy) is already solved here by a different mechanism
(snapshot/resume prefix cache).
